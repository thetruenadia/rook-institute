# EEB-1 — Weather and environmental design
Revision 2026-11-14. Working fictional design, not a climate simulation. Values are deliberately broad exploratory ranges; no probability distribution implied.

## Reference conditions
94 K, 1.7 bar, nitrogen/methane atmosphere, gravity 1.33 m/s², solar day ~11.4 Earth days, solar year ~7.3 Earth years. Approximately 36 W/m² incident stellar flux at top of atmosphere; actual surface flux and Bond albedo unresolved. Dense amber haze design, not calculated spectrum.

## Regional weather
| Region | Typical design temperature | Near-surface wind design | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern polar basin | 92–95 K | 0.3–2 m/s | Frequent cloud episodes, rain, sheltered fog, coastal breezes |
| Equatorial dunes | 94–97 K | 0.5–3 m/s | Long dry intervals, occasional methane storms, drifting organic particles |
| Eastern highlands | 91–95 K | 1–4 m/s | Orographic clouds, valley winds, local precipitation and exposed ridges |
| Southern lake district | 92–96 K | 0.3–2 m/s | Episodic rainfall, shifting shorelines, fog in locally saturated air |

These are selected worldbuilding ranges, not computed climatology. Storm gusts of 5–10 m/s may be explored, not established. Dense atmosphere makes low-speed winds potentially meaningful; dust transport thresholds and wave heights unresolved. Avoid assigning a global wind direction without circulation modelling.

## Daily and seasonal cycle
Long daylight and darkness with muted daily variations beneath haze. Temperature responds slowly in deep liquids and more rapidly in thin shallow pools. Polar seasons span years, with ~12 degree obliquity design; wet/dry shifts may occur but timing and hemispheric imbalance not established. Eclipse seasons on planet-facing regions may include up to ~4.2-hour central events. No automatic freezing or fog; methane saturation depends on local temperature, composition and humidity. Parent remains approximately fixed in sky; far hemisphere cannot see it.

## Distinctive weather proposals
Methane rain over ice bedrock; intermittent equatorial flash runoff; low clouds or fog near moist shores; organic dust drift; sheltered coastal pools with chemical layering; occasional nitrogen effervescence from mixing/warming/depressurization. No proven frequency or intensity. No global permanent rain or planet-wide orange opacity asserted.

## What a day feels like
Diffuse copper-toned daylight, soft shadows, dark dunes and pale ice exposures. Air is dense but unbreathable; liquids are hydrocarbons, not water. Small droplets and winds are candidates for distinctive motion. Visibility and colors are aesthetic decisions pending optical modelling.

## Next development
Map regional climate patterns qualitatively; design a few representative weather events and how each reshapes terrain; then connect habitat conditions to fictional indigenous biology.
